Contextual Credit Policy Optimization

Step-level credit for long-horizon LLM agents. CCPO weighs each observation-matched peer by how similar its context is, then credits a step for its history-conditioned return and for the progress realized shortly after. No value critic, no extra environment interactions.

Huiwen Han1,2,†, Ziyao Zeng3, Zihao Zhu2, Sisong Bei4, Hao Wang2, Hongyu Xu5, Yan Han6, Degui Zhi3, Zhiwen Fan2

TL;DR Group-based RL compares steps that saw the same observation. CCPO asks two further questions of every step: which peers actually share its context, and did the next few steps move toward success, or was the trajectory already on a good path?

Which peers should count?

Identical observations can occur after different preceding actions, reasoning, exploration depth and accumulated context. Peers in a group are not equally informative for a particular query.

→ Weight each peer by contextual similarity to the query.

Did this step make progress?

A favorable terminal outcome can hide redundant actions or detours inside a successful trajectory.

→ Pair history-conditioned return credit with the value change over a short realized continuation.

Contributions

  1. Dual-view contextual credit from history and future progress.Step-level credit combines a history-conditioned return residual with estimated value change over a future horizon, capturing outcome-relative performance and local progress.
  2. Contextual similarity for step-level peer weighting.Peer returns are aggregated with continuous weights set by the similarity between query and peer context representations, producing a context-dependent baseline.
  3. Semantic and structural context representations.Hidden states of recent interactions are combined with accumulated trajectory statistics, enabling context-sensitive comparisons without training an auxiliary value network.

Method

CCPO credits each step of a rollout without a value critic. It finds the steps in other rollouts of the same task that saw the same observation, and weights these peers by how similar their context is. The weighted peers answer two questions: did this trajectory’s return beat theirs (historical credit), and did the expected outcome rise over the next few steps (future credit)? Their sum is the step’s advantage.

Context φ
Frozen LLM hidden state + trajectory summary, each L2-normalized and concatenated with equal weights.
Windows
 KH = 2 history steps, KF = 2 future steps.
Training
150 iterations, 16 tasks × 8 trajectories, learning rate 10−6, γ = 0.95.
Overhead
No value critic to train and no extra rollouts; built on verl-agent.

Results

CCPO is evaluated on two multi-step benchmarks, ALFWorld (text-based household tasks) and WebShop (web shopping), with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. It is compared with off-the-shelf LLMs, prompt-based agents and RL training methods. At both model sizes, a CCPO variant has the highest mean success rate among the RL methods on ALFWorld and on WebShop.

The balance shifts from history toward future progress.

Historical credit dominates early. Future credit becomes more prominent later, even though the two are always combined with fixed unit weights. Within 10-iteration windows their magnitudes become less consistently correlated late in training, especially at 7B. The two components evolve differently, supporting complementary roles.

Changing the context representation changes when the agent acts.

Absolute changes in matched peer weights from context summaries

Context summaries add accumulated trajectory statistics to the semantic representation: step count, distinct-observation count, novelty fraction and a revisit indicator. Adding them changes normalized peer weights by more than one percentage point for 33.8% of matched query–peer pairs. Removing them lowers mean success by 2.6 points on ALFWorld and 1.3 on WebShop.

Limitations

  • Evaluation breadth.Two multi-step benchmarks and one model family. Broader generalization, and applicability to short-horizon or single-step tasks, remain untested.
  • Matching and hand-designed context.Exact observation matching can limit peer availability, while fixed temporal windows and hand-designed trajectory statistics may require adaptation across environments.
  • Sparse rewards.Future credit vanishes when all rollouts in a task group receive zero terminal reward, which limits the future channel early in training when successful trajectories are scarce.

Future work

  • Adaptive horizons and stage-dependent weighting.Motivated by the changing balance between historical and future credit over training.
  • Streaming and multimodal credit.Credit estimation from incomplete rollouts, and contexts that combine visual observations with textual histories.
  • Beyond exact matching.Semantic peer retrieval, and uncertainty estimates for correlated visits.
  • Broader evaluation.Generalization across model families and domains, computational scaling, and the effects of realized-future conditioning on policy updates.
